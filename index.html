Microfinance Unidos

Capital that comes back.

We are a nonprofit that studies how small-scale lending actually changes livelihoods — and puts that research to work. Funds raised through our benefit auctions are lent to entrepreneurs through Kiva, repaid by the borrower, and then lent again.

How the auction works What is microfinance?

Because microloans are repaid rather than spent, a single dollar raised can finance one business, then another, and then another. That compounding is the whole idea behind our work.

Repaid funds return to our Kiva account and are lent out again.

Why this matters

Talent is everywhere. Credit is not.

Most people who cannot borrow are not bad credit risks. They simply have no credit history, no collateral, and no bank branch willing to underwrite a two-hundred-dollar loan. The result is a working entrepreneur who cannot buy a second sewing machine, restock a shop, or replace a broken refrigerator.

What we do

Three parts, one purpose

We study the evidence, raise funds transparently, and lend where the money is needed.

We learn first

Microfinance is genuinely debated — some studies find meaningful gains in business investment and household resilience, others find modest effects on income. We read that literature honestly and publish what we find, including the parts that complicate the story.

We raise responsibly

Our fundraising runs on benefit auctions. Businesses contribute a product, a gift certificate, or a service; supporters bid; the proceeds become lending capital. Every donor receives written acknowledgement of exactly what they gave and what it raised.

We lend, not spend

Funds are lent to entrepreneurs through Kiva.org, a nonprofit lending platform operating since 2005. Because loans are repaid rather than consumed, the same capital goes back out to a new borrower again and again.

The mechanics

From an auction table to a market stall

The path from a donated item to a funded business is short and we can account for every step of it.

See the full process

  1. A business contributes an item

    A product, a gift certificate, or a service — whatever a business is comfortable giving. There is no cash contribution and no fee to participate.

  2. Supporters bid

    Items are catalogued, photographed, and presented with the contributing business named and credited throughout.

  3. Proceeds become lending capital

    Net proceeds go into our Kiva lending account. We publish the total raised and the loans it funded.

  4. Borrowers repay, and we lend again

    Repayments return to the account and are immediately re-lent. Nothing sits idle.

Our lending platform

Why we lend through Kiva

We are a small organisation. Rather than invent our own lending infrastructure, we use a platform that has been doing this carefully for two decades and that publishes its results in public.

  • Loans are listed individually, with the borrower's name, business, and purpose visible before you lend.
  • Lending starts at $25, so funds can be spread across many borrowers rather than concentrated in one.
  • Repayments flow back to the lending account, ready to be lent again.
  • Kiva reports its portfolio performance publicly, so our results can be checked rather than taken on trust.

For businesses

What a contributing business gets

Our auctions only work because businesses are willing to put something on the table. We take that seriously, and we make the exchange worth your while.

Named recognition

Your business is credited on the item listing, in the event programme, on this website, and in our post-auction report to supporters.

A real audience

Auction attendees are local supporters who are actively looking to spend money that evening. Contributing puts your product directly in front of them.

A clear paper trail

You receive a written acknowledgement describing the item, its stated value, the date received, and the amount it ultimately raised.
