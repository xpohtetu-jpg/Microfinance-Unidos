Contribute an item

For businesses

One item. A business financed on the other side of it.

We are asking local businesses to contribute a product, a gift certificate, or a service to our benefit auction. What it raises is lent to entrepreneurs through Kiva — and lent again every time it is repaid.

Contribute an item Read the questions first

The ask, stated plainly

What we are and are not asking for

We know that requests like this arrive constantly and that most of them are vague about what they actually want. So here it is without ambiguity.

We are asking for one item you already make, sell, or do — a product from your shelves, a gift certificate, an hour of your expertise — to be auctioned to supporters, with your business named throughout.

We are not asking for money, a sponsorship, a ticket purchase, advertising spend, a recurring commitment, or your customer list. There is no fee to participate and no minimum value.

What it costs you

Your wholesale cost, once. A $60 retail item might cost you $20 and raise $75 at auction. A service costs you an hour of time and frequently raises more than a physical item of the same stated value.

  • No participation fee
  • No minimum item value
  • No obligation to attend the event
  • No follow-up solicitation for money
  • No commitment to future auctions

In return

What a contributing business receives

These are commitments, not aspirations. If we fail to deliver any of them, tell us and we will fix it.

Named credit, everywhere

On the item listing with a link to your site, in the event programme, acknowledged from the front of the room, on our contributors page, and in the report sent to every supporter afterwards.

A written record

An acknowledgement letter describing the item, the value you stated, and the date received — issued when we confirm your contribution, not months later. Afterwards, a second note telling you what it raised.

Proof of where it went

The post-auction report shows gross proceeds, costs, the net amount lent, and the loans it funded. You can see the outcome of your contribution rather than being told it made a difference.

Your product in front of buyers

Auction attendees are there to spend. A gift certificate brings its winner through your door; a product introduces your work to a room of people who may not have encountered it.

One request, and only one

We ask once per auction cycle. If you decline, we do not follow up, we do not add you to a mailing list, and we do not pass your details to anyone else.

Control over how you appear

We send you the listing copy before it is published. If the description, the photograph, or the way your business is presented is not right, we change it.

Contribute

Tell us what you can offer

This takes about two minutes. Nothing here is binding — it starts a conversation, and you can change or withdraw the offer at any point before the catalogue closes.

If you would rather just talk it through, write to us at microfinanceunidos@gmail.com and we will answer any question before you commit to anything.

Not sure your item is suitable?

It almost certainly is. We have never turned down a contribution. If you are unsure whether something will attract bids, describe it and we will tell you honestly.

We link to this wherever your business is credited.

A rough figure is fine. We list what you tell us.

We use your details only to arrange this contribution and to send you the result. We do not share them, sell them, or add you to a mailing list.

Questions

What businesses ask us

Answered directly, including the ones with awkward answers.

Microfinance Unidos is a nonprofit initiative founded in 2026. We study the impact of microfinance and fund microloans through Kiva.org using the proceeds of benefit auctions. We are run entirely by volunteers and nobody draws a salary from it.

We are new, and we would rather say so than imply a track record we do not yet have. What we can offer instead is complete transparency about what we do with what we are given — see how we work for the specifics.

Every contributor receives a written acknowledgement recording the item, the value you stated, and the date we received it — the documentation your accountant will ask for.

Whether a contribution is deductible, and on what basis, depends on our registration status and on your own circumstances. Rather than make a claim on a web page, we will tell you exactly where we stand in writing when we confirm your contribution. Please treat your own tax adviser as the authority on how to treat it.

Unsold physical items are returned to you at our expense, or held for the next auction if you prefer — your choice, and we ask before doing either. Unredeemed gift certificates simply expire on whatever terms you set. You are never charged for an item that does not find a bidder.

No. You are warmly invited and there is no charge if you come, but plenty of contributors never attend and are credited exactly the same way. You will receive the result either way.

Yes, and most contributors do. Blackout dates, expiry dates, a reservation requirement, an exclusion on certain products, a minimum bid below which you would rather it not sell — all normal. We print your conditions on the listing and honour them exactly.

After each auction we publish gross proceeds, the direct costs of running it, the net amount lent, and the loans that money funded — including the countries and sectors. Contributors receive that report directly. Loans that are not repaid are reported as losses rather than omitted.

Lending happens through Kiva, whose loan records are public. Anyone who wants to check our figures against the platform is welcome to.

Only as you agree. By default we credit you by business name with a link to your website. If you would like us to use your logo, send it. If you would prefer no public credit at all, we will list the item as contributed anonymously. We send you the listing before it is published either way.

Then nothing at all happens. A simple no is a complete answer and we will not ask again this cycle, add you to a list, or pass your details on. Running a business is hard and there are seasons when giving inventory away is not sensible.

If you would like us to check back at a better time, say when and we will make a note of it.

One item takes two minutes to offer.

And it finances a business, then another, then another. If you have a question first, ask it — we answer every message.
