About us

A small organisation with a narrow, serious focus.

Microfinance Unidos exists to do two things well: understand what microfinance actually accomplishes, and move real money to entrepreneurs who cannot get it anywhere else.

Our mission

Study the evidence. Then act on it.

Microfinance has been described both as one of the most important poverty-reduction tools of the last fifty years and as an overpromised idea that has not delivered what its advocates claimed. Both positions are argued seriously by people who have looked closely at the data.

We founded Microfinance Unidos in 2026 because we thought that debate deserved more than a slogan. Our work begins with reading the research — including the studies that complicate the case for microlending — and continues by putting funds where the evidence points: toward working capital for entrepreneurs who already have a business and simply cannot finance its next step.

We raise those funds through benefit auctions, and we lend them through Kiva. Because the loans are repaid, the money does not stop working when the auction ends.

In one sentence

We turn contributed goods into lending capital, lend that capital to entrepreneurs through Kiva, and lend it again each time it is repaid — while publishing what we learn about whether it works.

What we are not

We are not a lender of record, a financial adviser, or an investment vehicle. Nobody who supports us receives a financial return. Money contributed to Microfinance Unidos is used for lending and for the modest costs of running an auction — nothing else.

Our principles

Four commitments we hold ourselves to

These are the standards we want to be judged against, including by the businesses that support us.

Report the whole picture

When we cite a study, we say what it found and what it did not. When a loan we funded is not repaid, we count it. Research that only ever confirms what we already believe is not research.

Respect the people who help us

Businesses that contribute to an auction are partners, not marks. We ask once, we ask clearly, we take no for an answer, and we credit everyone who says yes.

Account for every dollar

After each auction we publish the total raised, the costs incurred, the amount lent, and where those loans went. Anyone who contributed can see exactly what their item accomplished.

Lend, don't rescue

The people we lend to are running businesses, not waiting for charity. We fund the working capital they have already identified a use for, and we expect the loan back.

How we work

What a cycle looks like

We run on a repeating cycle rather than a permanent campaign. Each one has a clear beginning, a clear end, and a published result.

  • Research period

    We choose a question — the effect of loan size on business survival, say, or how repayment schedules affect borrowers — and read what has been published on it.

  • Auction build

    We invite businesses to contribute items, catalogue what comes in, and set a date.

  • The auction

    Items are presented and bid on. Contributing businesses are named throughout.

  • Lending

    Net proceeds are lent through Kiva. We select borrowers using what we learned during the research period.

  • Reporting

    We publish the numbers: raised, spent, lent, and where. Contributors receive the report directly.

  • Repayment and re-lending

    As loans are repaid over the following months, the funds are lent again. This continues indefinitely.

Who we are

The people behind it

Microfinance Unidos is run by a small volunteer team. Nobody draws a salary from the organisation.

Research

Reads and summarises the academic and field literature on microfinance, and decides what we lend toward.

Partnerships

Speaks with the businesses that contribute to our auctions, and is responsible for keeping every commitment we make to them.

Operations & reporting

Runs the auction itself, manages the Kiva lending account, and publishes the figures after each cycle.

Want to be involved?

We are always glad to hear from people who want to help with research, run an auction in their own community, or advise us. Send us a note and tell us what you would like to do.

Transparency

What we publish, and when

A new organisation has no track record, so the only reasonable thing to offer is a clear account of what we do with what we are given.

After every auction

Gross proceeds, costs incurred, net amount lent, and a list of the businesses that contributed.

Every lending round

The number of loans funded, the countries and sectors they went to, and the average loan size.

Annually

Cumulative totals, repayment performance including defaults, and how much capital has been re-lent.
