The benefit auction

Contributed goods, converted into lending capital.

Our fundraising model is deliberately simple. Businesses contribute something they already make or sell. Supporters bid on it. What the bidding raises is lent to entrepreneurs — and lent again each time it comes back.

Why an auction

It asks for the right thing from the right people

A donation drive asks businesses for money they have already budgeted elsewhere. An auction asks for something different: an item, a gift certificate, or a service — inventory a business already holds, or an hour of what it already does.

That distinction matters. The cost to the contributing business is its wholesale cost, not the retail price the item goes on to raise. Meanwhile the item is presented to a room of people who came out specifically to spend money that evening, with the business named on the listing. A contribution that costs a restaurant $40 in ingredients can raise several times that and put the restaurant in front of a hundred local people who had not been there before.

And unlike a straightforward donation, what the auction raises does not get spent once. It becomes a revolving fund.

Contribute an item

Nothing is asked of you financially

There is no fee to participate, no ticket purchase expected, no minimum item value, and no obligation to attend. If a contribution is not right for your business this season, that is a complete answer and we will not press it.

You set the terms

You decide what the item is, what it is worth, whether there are conditions on its use, and whether it expires. We list it exactly as you describe it and we do not alter those terms afterwards.

What works well

The kinds of contributions that raise the most

Almost anything can be auctioned, but some categories consistently draw stronger bidding than others.

Food, drink & hospitality

Dining certificates, tasting menus, catering for a small party, a coffee subscription, a case of something good. These are the reliable workhorses of any benefit auction.

Products & goods

Anything you make or stock — apparel, art, jewellery, home goods, specialty foods. A distinctive single item often outperforms a generic gift card of the same face value.

Services & expertise

A consultation, a lesson, a session, a repair, a portrait sitting. Services cost you time rather than inventory and frequently attract the most competitive bidding of the night.

Experiences

A behind-the-scenes tour, a private class, a table at an event you host, a workshop for four. Hard to price, easy to get excited about, and they cost you nothing to stock.

Gift certificates

Straightforward to issue and easy for bidders to value. They also bring the winner into your business afterwards, which is usually the point.

Bundles

Several businesses can combine into a single themed lot — a dinner, a bottle, and a car service, for instance. Bundles raise more than their parts and share the credit across every contributor.

The process

What happens after you say yes

Six steps, and we handle all of them except the first.

  1. You tell us what you can contribute

    A short form or a reply to an email. Describe the item, its approximate retail value, and any conditions. That is the entirety of the paperwork.

  2. We confirm and acknowledge in writing

    You receive a written acknowledgement recording the item, its stated value, and the date. Keep it for your records.

  3. We collect or you send it

    For physical items we arrange collection at a time that suits you. Certificates and services can simply be emailed.

  1. We catalogue and present it

    Photographed, described accurately, and listed with your business named and linked. We write the copy and send it to you before it goes out.

  2. The auction runs

    Bidding opens and closes on published dates. Winners collect or receive their items directly, and we handle the coordination.

  3. You get the result

    Within two weeks we send you what your item raised, what the auction raised in total, and how much of it went into lending.

Where the money goes

From the final bid to a funded loan

Auction proceeds cover the direct costs of running the auction — venue, printing, payment processing — and everything remaining becomes lending capital. We publish both figures, not just the flattering one.

Lending capital goes into our Kiva account and is disbursed in increments across multiple borrowers rather than concentrated in a single loan. As repayments arrive over the following months, they are lent out again without being withdrawn.

  • Nobody at Microfinance Unidos is paid from auction proceeds.
  • Costs are itemised in the report that follows each auction.
  • Repaid capital is re-lent, not returned to the organisation as income.
  • Loans that default are reported as losses rather than quietly omitted.

An illustration

Suppose an auction raises $4,000 and costs $400 to run. The remaining $3,600 is lent in $25 increments across a range of borrowers.

At a repayment rate near the 96% Kiva reports historically, the great majority of that returns over the following year and is lent again — and then again after that. Over several years, the same $3,600 can finance substantially more than $3,600 of business activity, shrinking gradually as defaults take their share.

This is an illustration of the mechanism, not a forecast. Actual results depend on the loans chosen and on how borrowers fare.

What we will publish

Gross raised · direct costs · net lent · number of loans · countries and sectors · repayment performance including defaults. Every contributing business receives it whether or not they attend.

Recognition

How contributing businesses are credited

Naming our contributors properly is not a courtesy. It is part of the arrangement.

On the listing

Your business name appears on the item description, with a link to your website.

At the event

Contributors are named in the programme and acknowledged from the front of the room.

On this website

A permanent contributors page listing every business that has supported an auction.

In the report

Named in the post-auction report sent to every attendee, supporter, and contributor.
